## Verdicts

1. **P1 SUPPORTED (interchange metric).** The causally selected frame transfers
   backtracking ~13× beyond diff-of-means at L17 (+0.649 vs +0.048; random +0.0003;
   misaligned-position swaps collapse the effect ~22×). Margins are three orders over
   the random floor; formal per-pair bootstrap CIs still owed (analysis-time follow-up),
   but no plausible CI overturns a 13× point gap.
2. **The causal frame is a DIFFERENT object, and a unique one.** Cold (random init) and
   warm (diff-means init) converge to the same axis, nearly orthogonal to diff-of-means.
   The causal criterion does not refine the correlational direction; it replaces it.
3. **Binary-variable signature confirmed.** shuffled_pair ≈ learned at all layers:
   within-class donor identity is irrelevant, as re-read in Amendment 2 (a shared
   behaviour-level feature, not pair-specific content). This arm is a positive
   diagnostic, not a null.
4. **The grounding controls split the layers — and catch the L27 trap.** Only L17
   passes everything: natural-coordinate separation AUC 0.760 AND 4.3× state-dependence.
   L11 (AUC 0.357) and L27 (AUC 0.211, worst state-dependence, biggest base-donor leak)
   yield high-transfer but UNGROUNDED frames — optimizer-carved interchange directions
   whose natural coordinate anti-separates the behaviour (the Makelov-style artefact,
   produced to order at the read-out-proximity layer). **Ranked by raw transfer alone,
   L27 would have won.** The coordinate-AUC control is what prevents that error.

## Bottom line
A causally selected, behaviourally grounded 1-D frame for backtracking exists at L17,
is nearly orthogonal to the diff-of-means direction, and transfers ~13× more per unit
intervention. L27's higher raw number is an artefact its own controls expose.

## TL;DR (read this first)

**Phase-7 — the causal steering experiment we agreed to run (last meeting's decision) — is done, end-to-end.** I generated 1,650 steered reasoning chains on RunPod, annotated them, and analysed them with a pre-registered de-confounded design (`Δ_floor`: a behaviour's own direction vs a *matched random control*). Then I stress-tested the result hard — including a 16-agent adversarial review and my own re-analysis — and the honest headline came out **narrower than the first pass suggested, which is the point of the exercise:**

1. **One clean, robust result: backtracking.** Suppressing reasoning along backtracking's own direction reduces backtracking **well beyond a matched random floor** (Holm-corrected p = 0.002), it is **behaviour-specific** (suppresses backtracking ~74% more than the other behaviours, p = 0.001), and — crucially — it **survives the obvious confound** (it is not just degeneration; see below). This is roughly *halving a rare behaviour*.
2. **A confound I caught and defused.** A second apparent hit (example-testing) turned out to be a **fluency-dilution artifact** — its steering made chains longer/loopier, shrinking the behaviour *fraction* without removing the behaviour. On a degeneration-immune metric it vanishes (p = 0.27). Finding and removing this is, I think, a strength of the write-up.
3. **The result is *preliminary* by construction, and I am now out of API budget.** The same annotator (Claude Sonnet) that built the behaviour labels also scored the steered chains, so nothing can be graded "PASS" until a *non-builder* annotator re-scores the chains. That re-annotation — plus one further control — is what I need a budget decision on.

## The result

**Design.** For each of 4 behaviours we steer along (a) its own difference-of-means direction and (b) its PCA-manifold subspace, each against a **matched random floor**, at the per-behaviour layers our attribution independently recovered. `Δ_floor = floor − arm` (positive ⇒ the real direction suppresses the behaviour *more than chance*). 50-task hold-out, paired BCa bootstrap, Holm across all 12 cells.

**Headline cells** (positive = behaviour suppressed beyond its matched random control):

| Behaviour / arm | fraction Δ (Holm p) | **count Δ — dilution-immune (Holm p)** | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **backtracking · own direction** | +0.054 (0.002) | **+6.7 sentences (0.007)** | ✅ robust |
| **backtracking · manifold** | +0.070 (0.002) | **+8.9 sentences (0.002)** | ✅ robust |
| example-testing · own direction | +0.060 (0.018) | +3.1 (0.27 — n.s.) | ✗ dilution artifact |
| uncertainty-estimation · all | ≈ 0 / negative | n.s. | undetermined (under-powered) |
| adding-knowledge · all | ≈ 0 | n.s. | genuine null |

**Why backtracking is trustworthy.** It is the *only* cell that survives on **three** independent endpoints (raw fraction, behaviour-sentence count, per-1,000-token rate) and on the fraction metric restricted to non-degenerate chains; its steered chains are *shorter and cleaner* than their floors (so degeneration works **against** it, not for it); and it is **behaviour-specific** — using the all-behaviour annotations, the backtracking vector suppresses backtracking ~74% more than the other three behaviours (p = 0.001). Effect size ≈ halving a rare behaviour (Cohen d_z ≈ 0.46–0.64). One honest nuance: backtracking and uncertainty are **geometrically entangled** (the backtracking vector also suppresses uncertainty), so the behaviours are not cleanly separable axes.

## Where this sits in the literature

- **Replicates Venhoff (2506.18167), and adds the control they lack.** Our attribution lands at the same Venhoff-faithful **middle layers (L15–L17)**, matching most of their per-behaviour choices (worth a quick check of their exact table before the meeting). But Venhoff reports *no* random/matched control — our `Δ_floor` is exactly that, and it **narrows** their qualitative "all four behaviours are controllable" to "backtracking, cleanly." (Notably uncertainty, which they highlight as steerable, fails our floor.)
- **Does *not* reproduce Huang's "manifold beats single direction" (2505.22411)** as a general law — consistent with the refusal literature's "more than one direction, but still one control knob." A useful, citable negative.
- **Orthogonal to the correctness-steering cluster** (LRS, ATLAS, CREST…): they steer toward accuracy; none tests per-behaviour specificity against a floor. That is our genuine niche.
- **Partial specificity is the textbook pattern** (Tan & Panickssery 2505.22637: ~⅓ of cases anti-steerable) — so "2 of 4 / 1 of 4 cleanly steerable" is expected, not a failure.

**Genuine contributions:** the matched-floor design for per-behaviour reasoning steering; quantified, multiplicity-corrected effect sizes (absent in the prior art); informative *nulls* the correctness cluster structurally cannot produce; and nulls cross-validated against our own standing geometry result (adding-knowledge fails the steering floor *and* the geometry specificity test).

## Honest limitations
1. **Circularity (the binding one):** Sonnet built the labels and scored the chains → preliminary by construction (this is *why* no cell is graded PASS). A dissociation argues against it being load-bearing, but only a non-builder annotator settles it.
2. **Floor calibration:** the manifold floors are dimension-matched but deliver less energy than their arms; the single-direction floor was calibrated on unperturbed text. The *direction* of the design is right; the realised calibration needs a fix.
3. **Single dose, greedy decoding:** one α, one sample/task — no dose-response curve yet, and greedy decoding is what let a few chains loop.
4. **Budget exhausted** ($560/$560). The annotation cost ~2.5× my estimate — output tokens are long and expensive, and a resume loop re-annotated long chains repeatedly; I've documented the fix.
